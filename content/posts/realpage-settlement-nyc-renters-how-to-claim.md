# RealPage Settlement: Can NYC Renters Claim? (Deadline January 29, 2027)

**If you paid rent on an apartment in the United States at any point between October 18, 2018 and November 21, 2025, and the building's management licensed RealPage's revenue management software (sold as LRO, YieldStar, or AI Revenue Management), you are in the settlement class. You check your address at RealPageRentalSettlement.com, and you must file a claim by January 29, 2027. You will need a document showing you personally paid rent at that address for at least one month.**

That last sentence matters more than it looks. A lot of pages about this settlement say no proof is needed. That is wrong, and believing it is the most common way people miss out. More on that below.

## What the settlement actually is

RealPage sells software that recommends rent prices to apartment owners and managers. Lawsuits alleged that the software pooled non-public information from competing landlords and used it to recommend prices, which reduced competition and pushed rents up. The claims trace back to a 2022 ProPublica investigation into the product then called YieldStar.

RealPage and a group of large property management companies agreed to pay about **$359.9 million** to resolve those claims. The companies deny wrongdoing, and a settlement is not an admission of guilt.

One timing note: as of this writing the settlement is not final. The court's final approval hearing is scheduled for **October 15, 2026**. The claim window is open in the meantime.

**What to do:** Treat this as real but pending. File your claim on time and expect the money later, not soon.

## Are you in the class?

Three conditions, all of which have to be true:

- **You paid rent yourself**, directly to the owner, manager, or owner-operator. Not your roommate paying for you, not a company paying on your behalf.
- **The lease was a multifamily residential lease** in the US or its territories.
- **The property was covered by a RealPage revenue management license** at the time, meaning LRO, YieldStar, or AIRM.
- **It happened between October 18, 2018 and November 21, 2025.**

You do not have to prove the algorithm raised your rent. You do not have to have complained at the time. Eligibility is about the property and the dates.

**What to do:** Make a list of every apartment you rented in that seven-year window, with the addresses and the months you lived there. You will use it in the next step.

## Why a New York City address might qualify

This is a national settlement, not a New York one, and that is exactly why NYC renters overlook it.

But RealPage's client list has included some of the largest operators in New York, among them Equity Residential, Greystar, and Cushman & Wakefield, according to reporting by [Brick Underground](https://www.brickunderground.com/rent/rent-setting-algorithm-software-increasing-rising-nyc-tenant-data-privacy-realpage-denies-collusion-price-fixing) and [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/05/20/realpage-rent-fixing-settlements-pass-200m/). If you rented in a large managed building rather than from a small private landlord, there is a real chance your address is on the list.

The pattern to expect: big amenity buildings and corporate-managed portfolios are the likely matches. A four-unit walkup owned by one family is not.

**What to do:** Do not guess from the building's size or your memory of the leasing office. Look the address up.

## How to check your address

The court-approved site is **RealPageRentalSettlement.com**. It carries the list of covered properties and the official claim form. Enter your former address and the dates you lived there, and the administrator checks it against the properties that were licensed for the software.

That is the whole test. There is no cost, and no other website can give you a real answer.

**What to do:** Check every address on your list, not just the most recent one. Several leases in the window means several potential claims.

## What you need to file, honestly

Here is where most coverage gets it wrong. This is **not** a no-documentation settlement. For each address you claim, the form asks for supporting documentation for at least one month showing that you personally paid rent there.

Things that generally serve that purpose:

- A signed lease with your name on it
- A bank or card statement showing a rent payment
- A rent receipt, a payment portal record, or a canceled check
- A money order stub

If you rented seven years ago and threw everything away, your bank's statement archive is usually the fastest path. Most banks keep several years of statements available to download.

**What to do:** Gather one document per address before you sit down to file. The form takes minutes when you have the paperwork and stalls for weeks when you do not.

## The three dates

- **September 1, 2026:** deadline to exclude yourself from the class or object. This has passed. If you did nothing, you are still in the class and can still file a claim. Only your right to sue separately is affected.
- **October 15, 2026:** the court's final approval hearing.
- **January 29, 2027:** the deadline to file a claim. This is the one that matters for you.

Filing a claim is the only way to receive money. Being eligible does nothing on its own.

**What to do:** Put January 29, 2027 in your calendar now, with a reminder a month earlier. Then file well before it.

## How much money is this, realistically?

Nobody can tell you a number yet, and anyone who quotes you one is guessing.

Payments are calculated pro rata, which means the fund is divided in proportion to what claimants paid. Renters who paid more rent over more months in the window get a larger share. The final per-person amount also depends on how many valid claims are filed nationwide, and millions of people are eligible.

Do the arithmetic yourself and you will see the shape of it: a $359.9 million fund, minus fees and costs, spread across millions of renters. For most people this will be a modest amount, not a windfall.

That is not a reason to skip it. It is a reason to file calmly, without planning around the money.

**What to do:** File because it costs you twenty minutes and the money is yours. Do not build a budget on it.

## What this changes about your next apartment

The uncomfortable part of this story is not the refund. It is what the lawsuits describe: while you were deciding whether $2,950 was a fair price for that one bedroom, the number on the other side of the table may have been produced by software with a view of the market that you did not have.

If that has ever made you feel like you were bad at this, or paranoid, or too slow, it is worth sitting with. You were not negotiating against a person's opinion of the apartment's worth.

The practical conclusion is not to negotiate harder. It is that the leverage a renter actually holds is knowing, before touring anything, which two or three things you will not give up. A price you cannot control is easier to face when you already know what you are buying and what you are not.

## Common questions

### I rented in NYC. Is this settlement for me?

Possibly. It is a national settlement covering properties whose management licensed RealPage revenue software, and several of the largest NYC managers have been RealPage clients. Your address either appears on the covered-property list or it does not, so look it up at RealPageRentalSettlement.com.

### I moved out years ago. Does that matter?

No. The class covers rent paid between October 18, 2018 and November 21, 2025. Where you live now is irrelevant. What matters is where you paid rent then and whether you can document it.

### Do I need a lawyer?

No. The claim is a form you fill out yourself, free. Class counsel is already appointed and paid from the fund. Any service offering to file for you in exchange for a cut is taking money you would otherwise keep.

### I had roommates. Can we all claim?

The class covers people who paid rent directly. If you each paid your share directly to the management company, each of you can file with your own documentation. If one person paid the full rent and the others reimbursed that person, only the person who paid the landlord has the direct payment record.

### I missed the September 1 deadline. Did I lose my chance?

No. September 1, 2026 was the deadline to opt out or object, not to claim. Doing nothing kept you in the class. Your claim deadline is January 29, 2027.

### Is the settlement money taxable?

Settlement payments can have tax consequences depending on your situation, and this is a question for a tax professional rather than a rental site. For most people the amount will be small enough that it is a minor issue, but do not take that as advice.
