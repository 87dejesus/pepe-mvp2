# Is That RealPage Settlement Email Real? How to Check Before You Click

**The RealPage rent-pricing settlement is genuine: about $359.9 million, covering renters who paid rent in the US between October 18, 2018 and November 21, 2025, with a claim deadline of January 29, 2027. The official claim site is RealPageRentalSettlement.com. Everything else follows four rules: filing is free, no one takes a percentage, no legitimate notice asks you to pay anything, and you should reach the site by typing the address yourself rather than clicking a link in an email.**

A real settlement with a public claim process and millions of eligible people is close to ideal conditions for fraud. The story checks out when the target searches it, which is the part that usually protects people.

## Why this one attracts copies

Most scams have to invent a reason for you to act. This one does not. The settlement is covered by real news outlets, the amount is real, and you probably did rent an apartment in that window.

So the fake version does not need to convince you of anything. It only needs to be the version you find first, or the one that lands in your inbox at the right moment. That is a much easier job.

**What to do:** Separate two questions that feel like one. "Is this settlement real?" and "Is this website real?" The first being true tells you nothing about the second.

## Rule 1: filing costs nothing, and nobody gets a cut

You file the claim yourself, free, on the official site. The lawyers who brought the case are already appointed by the court and paid out of the settlement fund.

So any service offering to file on your behalf for a fee, or for a percentage of whatever you receive, is charging you for a form you can complete in twenty minutes. That is not always fraud in the legal sense. It is always a bad deal.

**What to do:** If the words "we handle the filing" or "success fee" or "we take X percent" appear anywhere, close the tab and go to the official site.

## Rule 2: "no proof needed" is the tell

A striking number of pages about this settlement promise that no documentation is required. It is the most repeated claim about it online, and it is wrong.

For each address you claim, the official form asks for supporting documentation showing that you personally paid rent there for at least one month: a lease, a bank statement, a payment portal record, a receipt.

Sites that lead with "no proof needed" are optimizing for clicks, not accuracy. Some are harmless content farms. Some are collecting your personal information. Either way, a page that gets the central requirement backwards should not be the page you trust with your address history.

**What to do:** Treat "no proof needed" as a signal that the page has not read the settlement. Verify anything it told you elsewhere.

## Rule 3: type the address, do not click the link

You may receive a legitimate notice about this settlement. Class notices do go out by email and postal mail. You may also receive a very good imitation of one.

You do not have to tell them apart, and that is the point. Both lead to the same place, so ignore the link and go there yourself. Type RealPageRentalSettlement.com into the address bar.

If the email was real, you lost nothing. If it was not, the attack never got started.

The details worth noticing, if you want to look anyway: an address that is close but not exact (extra words, a different ending, a hyphen that should not be there), a demand that you act within hours, and any request for a payment method. A settlement pays you. It never needs your card.

**What to do:** Never reach a claim form through an email link. The five seconds of typing is the entire defense.

## Rule 4: know which information is normal to give

A real claim form needs enough to identify you and match your tenancy: your name, contact information, the addresses you rented, the dates, and your documentation. For payment, an administrator may need tax information, and that is normal for settlement payouts.

What is not normal, at any point:

- A bank login, or a request to "verify" your account by signing in through their page
- A payment, a fee, or a card number
- A gift card or crypto transfer of any kind
- A request over the phone from someone who called you first

That last one deserves its own line. If someone calls claiming to be from the settlement administrator and asks you to confirm details, hang up and go to the official site. Legitimate administrators do not cold-call people for account information.

**What to do:** Give identifying information only on a site you navigated to yourself. Give money to no one.

## If you already gave something up

Move in this order:

- **Card or bank details:** call your bank now and have the card frozen. Speed matters more than certainty.
- **A payment you sent:** contact your bank or the payment app immediately and ask about a reversal. Report it to the [FTC](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).
- **Login credentials:** change that password, and change it anywhere you reused it.
- **Only your name and address:** you are most likely fine. Expect more targeted attempts and be skeptical of anything referencing your old apartment.

Then file the real claim. Getting phished does not remove you from the class.

**What to do:** Handle the exposure first, in that order, then go to the official site and file properly.

## Common questions

### What is the official RealPage settlement website?

RealPageRentalSettlement.com is the court-approved site. It hosts the covered-property list and the claim form. Reach it by typing the address rather than following a link you were sent.

### I got an email about the settlement. Is it fake?

It might be real. Class notices are sent by email and mail. But you never need the link to act, so do not use it. Go to the official site directly and check your address there.

### Is there a fee to file a claim?

No. Filing is free. Class counsel is paid from the settlement fund, not by you. Anyone charging you a fee or a percentage is selling you something you can do yourself.

### Do I really need documents?

Yes. For each claimed address you need documentation showing you personally paid rent for at least one month. Pages that say otherwise are wrong. A bank statement showing a rent payment is usually the easiest to retrieve.

### Someone called me about my settlement claim. Is that normal?

Treat an incoming call as untrusted, always. Do not confirm details, do not give account information, and do not follow instructions from that call. Hang up and go to the official site.

### How do I know if my old NYC building is covered?

Look the address up on the official site. Guessing from the building's size or management company is unreliable, and the lookup is free and immediate.
