# Demo moments

Designing for demonstrability, without letting it distort the product.

## The problem this solves

Alarm apps go viral because the demo is visually absurd in six seconds — Wayk's
pushups, the photo of the sink, the sprint around the house. Reading a calendar
list is not watchable. That was the single largest growth risk in the original
concept.

## What is watchable here

1. **Your own voice at 6am.** The alarm plays the user, from last night, saying
   why they're getting up. The reaction shot is the content. This is the
   strongest asset the product has and it exists for mechanism reasons, not
   marketing ones (ADR-0011).
2. **The moment of recognition** — the face on realising there's a 7am.
3. **Getting it wrong.** Tapping the wrong promise, half asleep, is funny and it
   demonstrates the mechanism better than any explanation.
4. **The overnight lock-screen recording** from SPIKE-001. Unglamorous, real,
   and the most honest artefact we will have for weeks.

## Rule

**No feature exists for the camera.** If a demo moment requires a product change
that no hypothesis justifies, it does not ship. Demonstrability is a tiebreaker
between equally good designs, never an argument on its own.
