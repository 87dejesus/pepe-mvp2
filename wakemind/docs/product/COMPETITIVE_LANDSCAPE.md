# Competitive landscape

Living document. Detail and sources in `discovery/DISCOVERY_ROUND_1.md` §6.

## Direct — mission alarms

**Alarmy** — roughly USD 500K/month, ~500K downloads/month. Already ships a
time-and-weather voice briefing and a "Wake Up Check". One sprint away from a
calendar feature. Fixed missions, therefore decays.

**Wayk** — launched Feb 2026, 25M TikTok views and 100K downloads in 30 days,
#15 App Store. Proof that the category is won by demonstrability, not
intelligence. Also fixed missions.

## The calendar-alarm graveyard

OnTimer, Calalarm, Alarmate, Today Planned, Calendar Alarm, AlarmCal, Cannot
Ignore, and others. **A dozen apps have shipped "your calendar in your alarm."
None broke out.**

This is the most important competitive fact we have, and it is a constraint on
our positioning: the calendar is not the differentiator. The mechanism is.

## Indirect

**Apple** — iOS already shows a Good Morning screen with weather and calendar
after alarm dismissal under Sleep Focus. Passive, and it has changed nobody's
behaviour, which is the opening. Apple can deepen it in one release.

**Sleep Cycle** — 878K paying subscribers and declining ~2% QoQ. A useful
ceiling estimate for a best-in-class sleep-adjacent subscription app.

## Our position

Everyone attacks the body — louder, harder, more physical. Nobody attacks the
automatic response itself. That lane is empty, and ADR-0007 is why we can hold
it: self-authored content is not a feature a competitor can copy without
becoming a different product.
