# TikTok pilot: Opposite right answers

Status: draft for founder review
Date: 2026-09-01
Format: 5-photo TikTok carousel
Audience: NYC renter mid-search, lease deadline inside roughly 60 days, overwhelmed by tradeoffs

## Why this concept

The recent feed often states the pain as a broad truth. This pilot instead shows a recognizable decision moment and lets the renter discover the lesson.

The creative uses one real tension from the customer-voice bank: two renters can judge the same apartment differently because a dealbreaker is personal. The villain is not the apartment or the renter. It is using someone else's list while the market creates pressure to decide.

## Test hypothesis

This story format should outperform generic advice because it combines:

1. An open loop: why did two people make opposite decisions?
2. A real renter phrase, not synthetic pain language.
3. A visual tradeoff the viewer understands without explanation.
4. A product mechanism that appears only after the insight: decide Your Lines first.

## Measurement

Primary business metric:

- UTM visits from TikTok and emails captured.

Creative leading indicators:

- Profile visits per 1,000 views.
- Shares and saves per 1,000 views.
- Carousel completion or average photos viewed, if TikTok exposes it.

Do not decide from one post. Run three story-led carousel pilots over two weeks, then compare the group with the recent generic-statement posts. Keep the same CTA and UTM so the creative angle is the main variable.

## Next two pilot cores

1. **The Tuesday test:** the apartment looked perfect during the tour, then a normal Tuesday exposed the commute, light, and kitchen tradeoffs.
2. **Identity is not control:** a renter verifies who a subletter is but not whether that person controls the apartment. The story begins with relief and reveals the missing check.

Both should keep the same architecture: scene, tension, hidden problem, new rule, light CTA.
